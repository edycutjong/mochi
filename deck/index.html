The problem, stated once

Presence is the
only content.

And presence is scarce. Decentraland worlds are venues — wonderful when full, dead when empty, which is almost always.

Say the headline out loud and then stop talking for a beat. The diagram does the rest: two real people, thirteen hours apart, in the same room. Every social feature Decentraland ships assumes that gap is zero. It never is.
Why the obvious fixes don't work

Every patch needs a
second person, right now.

A person arriving alone finds an empty couch, and nothing says other people were here and cared about this.
02:10, alone

Someone opens Decentraland on their phone at 2am because they can't sleep. The world they pick is a beautiful, empty room. They look around for eleven seconds and close the app.

This is not a content problem or a graphics problem. It is a scheduling problem, and it is the platform's own retention problem stated plainly: there is no reason to open a venue twice.

Source: specs/spec.md — the problem statement this project was scoped against.

What everybody tries
01

Schedule an event

Needs a crowd to show up at a fixed hour, in a fixed timezone.

02

Hire a host

Needs a paid human awake at 2am, and stops the moment the money does.

03

Add a chat box

Needs somebody on the other end to answer. An empty room has nobody.

All three fail identically: they require other people to be online at the same moment as you — exactly the thing an empty world cannot supply.

Walk the three patches quickly — the judges have all built one of them. The payoff is the dark card: name the shared failure mode as synchrony. Then pivot: “so I stopped trying to fill the room.”
The solution

The creature is the log.

No counter beside the blob. No leaderboard. The record of who cared for it is the shape of its body.

Mochi is a giant pastel blob co-parented by every visitor. Feed it and it is physically bigger from then on — for everybody, forever, whether or not you are still online.

The growth rule, as shipped
scale = 1.000 + 0.012 × feeds  (max 1.9)

Server-authoritative. The scene owns no state, keeps no counter and has no delete verb — nothing anyone contributes can be taken back out.

Creativity & originality

Four ways a stranger stays.

Each one is a different grammar of credit — a quantity, an ordered list, a name with a timestamp, and a message addressed to you.
01

Its size is the sum

The blob is bigger because people fed it. Not a number beside it — the actual body. Hunger decays over 36 h to a floor of 0.15, so an untended Mochi looks untended, and can never starve.

perFeed 0.012 · base 1.0 · max 1.9 · floor 0.15
02

Its dance is a credited chain

Each move was taught by one named person. When Mochi performs, it replays the whole chain in order, crediting every move, beside ghost dancers wearing those people's real wearables.

replaying all 7 moves  →  the chain is yours now
03

Its plaque names the last human

Deliberately coarse. “3h ago” carries the feeling that somebody was here and has since gone; “2h 47m ago” reads like a log file and carries nothing.

last fed by Kito
3h ago
04

The away-line finds you

On your next arrival, Mochi names the person whose act followed yours. It is the one message in the whole protocol that is never broadcast — it is addressed to a single returning visitor.

Rue fed Mochi after you left.

Naming note: Kito and Rue are this repo's test-fixture wallets, shown here as the literal string format. They are not real carers — see slide 11 for the real count.

Slow down on the fourth card. Feeding and plaques exist elsewhere; a message that waits for one specific person to come back does not. That is the card that answers “why would anyone return.”

How it works

The scene owns no state.

It sends intents and renders what the server says. One process, one SQLite file, one creature — correct for that, and honest that it does not shard.
Decentraland Mobile · SDK7 scene

mochi/replay.ts · dancers.ts · plaque.ts
mochi/teach.ts · meadow.ts · ui/controls.ts
net/client.ts backoff 1s → 20s · intent queue capped at 8

Losing the connection never clears the screen.

lastState survives a disconnect on purpose, and no error notice is shown — it would only mark a gap the visitor cannot act on.

Zero imported GLB/GLTF. 26 Tween + 9 TweenSequence, zero Animator.

WebSocket · protocol.ts
intents ▸
hello · feed · teach{emoteId, wearables} · pet · stamp
◂ state
pet row · last 40 chain moves · today's carers

protocol.ts has zero imports — both sides import the same file, so client and server cannot disagree about what a message is.

Rate limits per wallet per 60s — feed 4 · teach 2 · pet 10 · stamp 2.
Guests render everything and are refused writes.

Node 22.5 · ws · Fly.io
ws.ts  —  socket + framing
game.ts  —  rules, limits, guests
store.ts  —  queries
node:sqlite — three tables
pet ×1 chain_move carer_event

append-only · teacher_name NOT NULL · no delete verb exists

Hunger is derived, never ticked.

It is computed from a timestamp, so there is no cron, and the value is correct after any amount of downtime.

Two claims to make here and no more: the client is a renderer, and the log is append-only with no delete path at any layer. If a judge only remembers one architectural fact, make it “hunger is derived from a timestamp” — it is why downtime cannot corrupt the creature.
Mobile-first · UX & accessibility

Designed for a thumb,
not shrunk to fit one.

Judged criterion #1, and first in the published tie-break order. Every decision below exists because the target device is a phone held in one hand.

No buttons, ever

FEED is the bowl, TEACH is the stage, PET is a hold on the body, STAMP is a tap on the totem. Device testing found a bottom thumb arc sitting on the client’s own joystick and jump control, so the arc was deleted, not nudged.

HUD is a status line — the bottom of the screen is the client’s

Zero typing, zero tutorial

Identity comes from the wallet. There is no onboarding modal and no instruction text, because judges arrive as players, not as readers.

No gesture can fail

Hold-to-pet latches on press and the release is watched globally, so a sliding thumb cannot cancel it. There is no fail state anywhere in the scene — a fat-thumb gesture that can fail is an accessibility defect.

tap + press only · no drag, pinch or traced shape

Authored at mobile scale

Sized in virtualWidth / virtualHeight, never raw pixels; desktop layout ×3 for mobile per Decentraland's own guidance; wrapped in ScreenInsetArea for notch and home indicator.

Accessibility floor

≥ 18sp in-scene text, ≥ 22sp on buttons
≥ 4.5:1 contrast on every text run
Nothing depends on sound — the mobile client has no audio events
No red/green-only signalling
No timed input, anywhere
Complete visit in 30–60s, one-handed, portrait
Crosshair hidden, joystick kept — the core loop survives standing still
Land one sentence hard: there is no fail state in this scene. Then note the spawn point frames the creature centre-screen, so a judge who never touches the joystick still sees the whole thing.

What isn't true yet

The honest slide.

This deck is going to be read next to a public repository. Everything below is already written down in JUDGE.md — you should hear it from me first.

Known limits, shipped

Emote observation is unverified on mobile

Catching a move performed with the client's own emote wheel is not confirmed on a phone. The 12-tile picker is the shipped fallback, so what is at risk is spontaneity, not the mechanic.

Audio is decorative

The Decentraland mobile client has no audio event implementation. Nothing in the experience depends on sound.

One process, one SQLite file

Correct for one creature and a few hundred carers. It does not shard, and it is not built to.

History was bootstrapped

Seeded rows carry an is_seed flag in the database and are labelled as such. The server refuses guest writes.

One axis of state

Hunger only — not a mood system. A deliberate cut, so the loop stays legible in eight seconds.

The ask

Visit twice, hours apart.

That is the entire test. If the second visit is different because of somebody you never met, the thesis holds. If it isn't, it doesn't.

For the jury

Open it on your phone, alone, at an odd hour — the condition it was built for. Feed it, teach it one move, close the app. Come back later and read what it says to you.
